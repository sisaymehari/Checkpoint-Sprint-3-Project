# Testing

This file explains how we tested each rubric point.

- **Unit tests:** run `npm i && npm test`. The tests are in `common.test.mjs`.
- **Manual tests:** done in the browser, using the month and year dropdowns and the Previous / Next buttons.
- **Accessibility tests:** Lighthouse in Chrome DevTools, **Snapshot** mode, **Desktop** device, Accessibility category.

---

## Rubric: for everyone

### 1. Open the calendar, it should be showing the current month

**Manual test.** Opened the page and checked that the heading, the month dropdown and the year dropdown show today's month and year (October 2026), and that the grid shows the days of that month.

### 2. Go to October 2024

**Manual test.** Chose October and 2024 in the dropdowns and checked against a real calendar:

- The grid has 5 rows × 7 columns, with Sunday in the first column.
- The first row has 2 blank boxes (Sunday and Monday), then Tuesday 1 to Saturday 5.
- The last row has Sunday 27 to Thursday 31, with no boxes after it.
- October 8th shows Ada Lovelace Day.
- October 25th shows World Lemur Day.

**Unit tests in `common.test.mjs`:**

- `finds the second Tuesday of October`: Ada Lovelace Day 2024 is the 8th.
- `finds the last Friday of October`: World Lemur Day 2024 is the 25th.
- `lists the special days of one month`: `getCommemorativeDays(2024, "October", daysData)` returns Ada Lovelace Day on the 8th and World Lemur Day on the 25th.

### 3. Open October 2020

**Manual test.** October 13th shows Ada Lovelace Day and October 30th shows World Lemur Day.

**Unit tests in `common.test.mjs`:** `finds the second Tuesday of October` (13 in 2020) and `finds the last Friday of October` (30 in 2020).

### 4. Open May 2030

**Manual test.** May 11th shows International Binturong Day.

**Unit test in `common.test.mjs`:** `finds the second Saturday of May 2030` (11).

### 5. Months start and end on the right dates, with no extra boxes

**Manual test.** Opened each month and checked the first and last day, and the empty boxes:

| Month   | First day                | Last day                 | Result |
| ------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------ | ------ |
| 2024-12 | Sunday, 0 empty before   | Tuesday, no boxes after  | ✅     |
| 2025-02 | Saturday, 6 empty before | Friday, no boxes after   | ✅     |
| 2025-05 | Thursday, 4 empty before | Saturday, no boxes after | ✅     |
| 2026-02 | Sunday, 0 empty before   | Saturday, only 4 rows    | ✅     |

We only draw empty boxes **before** the 1st, so there are never extra boxes after the last day.

### 6. Previous and next buttons, including past the ends of the selector

**Manual test.**

- Clicked Next from October 2026 to January 2027: the year changes and the grid is correct.
- Clicked Previous from January to December of the year before.
- Clicked many times in both directions: the heading, the grid and both dropdowns always match.
- Went to December 2100 and clicked Next: January 2101 is shown. The year dropdown shows 2101 (not empty), because the year list is rebuilt around the shown year every time the calendar is drawn.
- Never saw an empty value, `undefined`, `null` or `NaN` in the UI.

### 7. A usable way of jumping to a month and year

**Manual test.** Used the month and year dropdowns to jump to October 2024, October 2020 and May 2030. The calendar changes as soon as a value is picked.

### 8. UI is generated by DOM manipulation, and day generation is dynamic

**Code check.** `index.html` only has an empty `<div id="calendar">` inside `<main>`. Everything else (heading, weekday row, day boxes, special days, dropdowns, buttons) is created in `web.mjs` with `document.createElement`. The special days come from `days.json` through `getCommemorativeDays`; no day is hard-coded.

**Unit test in `common.test.mjs`:** `shows a day that was added to the data file` adds International Dawn Chorus Day (first Sunday of May) and checks it is found on May 4th 2025, without changing any code.

### 9. 100% accessibility in Lighthouse (Desktop) for all views

**Lighthouse test** (Snapshot mode, Desktop, Accessibility). Scored 100 on:

- October 2026 (current month)
- October 2024, October 2020, May 2030
- September 2024 (longest special day name)
- January 2026 (no special days)
- December 2024, February 2025, February 2026
- January 2101 and January 1900

### 10. Unit tests for at least one non-trivial function

**Unit tests in `common.test.mjs`** for `getDateFromRule`, `getCommemorativeDays` and `getEventsForYears`. They check "second", "first" and "last" rules, including the edge cases where the wanted weekday is the 1st or the last day of the month. Run with `npm test`.

### 11. No dead code

**Code check.** Every function, variable, import and CSS rule is used. No placeholder comments from the starter project are left.

## Rubric: for groups of at least 2

### 12. `node` script generates `days.ics`, and the dates match in Google Calendar

**Manual test.**

1. Ran `node generate-ical.mjs`. It created `days.ics` with 55 events (5 days × 11 years, 2020 to 2030).
2. Imported `days.ics` into a new empty test calendar in Google Calendar.
3. Checked the same dates as the web page: October 2024 (Ada Lovelace 8th, World Lemur 25th), October 2020 (13th and 30th) and May 2030 (Binturong 11th). ✅

**Unit test in `common.test.mjs`:** `makes one event per day per year` checks there is one event per day per year from 2020 to 2030, and that the first event is Ada Lovelace Day on 2020-10-13.

### 13. Whole-day events with no start or end time

**Manual test.** In Google Calendar, every imported event shows as "All day".

**Code check.** Each event uses `DTSTART;VALUE=DATE:YYYYMMDD` (a date with no time), with no end time and no repeating rule.

### 14. Date logic is shared between the web page and the iCal script

**Code check.** All date calculations are in `common.mjs`:

- The web page (`web.mjs`) uses `getCommemorativeDays`.
- The iCal script (`generate-ical.mjs`) uses `getEventsForYears`.
- Both of these use the same function, `getDateFromRule`.
